# Frameflow — video production with accountable evidence

This is an initial workflow skill prepared for release, not a video-generation platform. Use it with the owner's chosen editor, renderer, asset tools and reviewers. No backend, model, language, visual style, paid service or publishing destination is implied. Installing this skill does not authorize account access, spending, external transfers or release.

## 1. Establish the working contract

Read the actual latest brief and current files. Resolve audience, purpose, duration range, aspect ratio, delivery formats, language, accessibility needs, factual claims, creative freedoms, approved assets and non-negotiable constraints. Identify who owns the work and who can authorize cost or distribution. Preserve pauses and previously approved material. Use `templates/workflow.md` to fill gaps, not to replace an existing authoritative production record.

Select a mode explicitly:
- **plan**: investigate and create planning material; do not quietly render or publish.
- **review**: inspect fixed supplied material; do not modify the source.
- **revise**: change only the authorized scope, then review affected material and regressions.
- **production**: execute the agreed brief through locally or explicitly authorized delivery.

Configure tools, models, review requirements, budget limits and destinations per project. If a required tool is unavailable, distinguish reversible preparation from the blocked operation. Report the exact missing input, capability or authorization. Continue independent authorized work. A requested result is not permission to exceed cost, rights or privacy boundaries.

## 2. Write the story before optimizing a checklist

State what changes for the viewer or protagonist. Write the full narrative or argument in plain language before dividing it into shots. For a character-led piece, establish desire, action, obstacle, discovery and consequence. For an explanatory piece, establish the question, evidence, explanation and useful limit. For a mood or music-led piece, define the developing sensory idea and why its progression matters.

Do not force a universal act count, shot count, pacing formula or camera-emotion mapping. Keep imagined scenes distinct from documented events; do not turn a concept animation into a product capability claim. Locate sources for factual statements and record uncertainty. Resolve a weak story before compensating with effects.

## 3. Convert intention into executable direction

For each shot record its narrative purpose, visible action, performance, staging, camera, start/end state, duration estimate, sound, text and asset dependencies. Describe what the viewer can actually perceive, rather than only the intended feeling. Specify transitions and continuity: screen direction, gaze, prop state, position, lighting, movement and audio bridges.

Create a small storyboard or animatic with temporary materials clearly labeled. Test comprehension, coverage and timing before expensive asset creation. A storyboard can validate composition but cannot certify final motion, acting, sound or pacing. Revise the director plan when the story requires new coverage rather than asking an editor to invent missing action.

## 4. Prepare assets and claims

Inventory each asset's source, owner, license or permission, permitted uses, revision and exact bytes. Separate authentic product views, illustrative mockups, fictional characters and synthetic voices. Do not substitute reconstructed marks or lookalikes for approved identity assets. Keep private reference material out of portable packages.

Choose acquisition, drawing, procedural construction, filming, generation or editing by the actual need and authorized tools. Record inputs and operations sufficient to reproduce or diagnose the result without storing secrets. Examine assets at their intended display size; inspect edges, anatomy or geometry, consistency, typography and factual details as applicable. Preserve approved originals; make versioned derivatives for crop, translation, compression or retouching.

## 5. Animate, edit and build sound together

Choose the pacing source for the film: dialogue, action, music, observation or a mixture. For dialogue-driven work, approve the actual spoken take and timing before locking dependent performance. For nonverbal work, do not manufacture narration merely to fit a pipeline.

Build motion from motivated start/end states, readable anticipation, action and consequence. Check contact, weight, continuity and camera movement where relevant. Use previews to isolate hard shots before full rendering. Fast pacing should create meaningful changes, not merely more cuts or particles.

Assemble a rough cut for story, then refine performance, rhythm, transitions and visual consistency. Design sound with intelligibility, space, dynamics and intentional silence. Validate captions against the current spoken track and the actual frame timing. Waveforms, transcripts and alignment can support timing checks; they cannot demonstrate natural pronunciation, expressive performance or a satisfying mix. Listen to the real current mix and watch the film at normal speed.

## 6. Freeze and independently review

Before dispatching a review, freeze actual artifacts and their dependency versions. Identify the project-selected rubric, required dimensions, reviewer count and independence requirements. Provide a fresh reviewer with the real work and minimal audience context, initially withholding author explanations, past scores and desired conclusions. Preserve first-view impressions before supplying the rubric and factual evidence.

A reviewer must actually access the medium they assess. Record concrete locations, observed effects, supporting examples and limitations. Mark unobserved or unavailable dimensions **NE** (not evaluated). Viewing a still is not a motion review; reading a transcript is not listening. Do not create nominally separate reviewers by assigning several role names to one context. If independent review is unavailable, state that gate as blocked rather than self-signing it.

The local CLI checks manifest structure, referenced bytes and declared obligations only. It cannot authenticate a human reviewer, perceive a film, establish real independence or award artistic quality. Compare the configured target against actual observed dimensions outside the CLI. Never present integrity verification as a quality pass.

## 7. Diagnose, repair and resume safely

Classify each failure: story, direction, asset, motion, edit, sound, factual claim or technical delivery. Record the location, likely cause, dependencies and a result-based retest. Preserve good regions as regression controls. Make the smallest sufficient reversible change; retain the previous version and failed review.

Invalidate evidence for changed bytes and affected dependents. Reuse a review only when its bytes, purpose, dependencies and evaluation scope remain unchanged. Two unsuccessful attempts of the same kind call for a different diagnostic test before another blind parameter change. Do not erase a failure simply by changing the record to pending or updating a hash.

At a pause, record the current artifact versions, completed operations, unresolved findings, pending obligations, last known external operation and next authorized step. On resume, inspect reality before trusting a stale status. If an external result is unknown, check the original operation or receipt before retrying to avoid duplicate charges or submissions.

## 8. Deliver honestly and learn

Check the actual export, not just the timeline: duration, dimensions, frame rate, audio presence, caption timing, color and intended playback compatibility. Inspect first and last frames, scene boundaries and sensitive details. Preserve the reviewed master and a manifest of delivery derivatives. A byte-changing derivative may require targeted rechecking.

Deliver only to the authorized destination and audience. For an authorized upload, read back the saved item and verify accessible correct bytes before declaring delivery. Publication is a distinct permission and status. Report separately what was designed, executed, independently reviewed, target-attained, delivered and published. List unresolved limitations without implying success.

Capture one useful lesson with observation, evidence, cause hypothesis, repair, unchanged controls and measured result. State where it applies and how to test it next time. A local lesson is not automatically a universal production rule.
